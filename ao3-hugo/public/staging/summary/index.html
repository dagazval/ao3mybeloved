“Shut up,” she said, her rough Ala Mhigan contralto cutting right through his grand address. “I know it’s you under that stupid hood, G’raha Tia.”

The silence that followed was absolute. And as thick as a thousand-year-old tree.

I can’t be the only person whose immediate reaction to the Crystal Exarch opening his mouth was, “Oh, I know exactly who you are.”

Vex crosses the rift straight out of the bloody trench-mud of Ghimlyt expecting an imperial ambush or an early grave. What she gets instead is a dying world drowned in blinding light, missing comrades, and an archivist playing savior in a dramatic bathrobe. She has three pounds of lead in her soles, a mouthful of fangs, and zero patience for a century-old martyr complex dressed up as civic duty. But saving Norvrandt means reckoning with what eighty-odd years did to him—and the feelings she buried when the iron doors of the Syrcus Tower first slammed shut.

So here we are: a retelling of Shadowbringers through Endwalker from the lens of the Champion clocking him five seconds in, weaving canon events through heavy headcanon, unhurried romance, and a lot of personal processing of Emotional Damage.

And because love rarely stays inside a neat, tidy box: this is an eventual slow-burn, non-hierarchical polyamorous triad. The First belongs to Vex and G’raha finding each other across the dark; the return to the Source brings Dionysus Locke into the fold, where a gentle, unexpectedly pansexual scholar and a grounded, watchful Viera might just figure each other out long before the stray brawler finishes digging herself out from under her mountain of trauma.

There is ANGST, there are sharp teeth, and yes—I play a Miqo’te and I am a G’raha simp. Fanservice all the way down.

This thing is long, messy, and mostly written for friends and my own catharsis, but if you’re a random stranger tumbling in from the tag filters: welcome, mind the content warnings, and please be kind in the comments. [Which I may eventually enable on this site.]
