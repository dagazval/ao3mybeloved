Prologue: The Folly of Best Laid Plans

2026-11-03 00:00:00 +0000 UTC

The mud in the Ghimlyt Dark had the consistency of wet lard and smelled of spent ceruleum.

It was a miserable, indecisive kind of cold—the sort that didn’t have the decency to freeze the ground solid, just spit sleet into your collar until your leathers were sodden and your shins ached. Around the revetment, the trench timber shuddered under the rhythmic, concussive hammering of imperial magitek artillery half a malm north.

Vex wiped a smear of greasy soot from her jaw with the back of her wrist, her breathing shallow behind her teeth. Her boots—heavy, lead-weighted brawler soles caked in two inches of grey muck—stayed planted wide in the slush.

To her left, Raubahn’s broadsword took an imperial vanguard clean through the chassis, his roar cutting through the scream of tearing iron. “Rally to the second trench! Move!”

“Moving,” Vex muttered.

She didn’t run. She shifted her stance, knuckles tightening inside the brass dusters. An imperial shock trooper cleared the mud parapet, bayonet leveled. Vex didn’t waste the breath to yell. She stepped inside his reach, knocked the barrel wide with her forearm, and drove three pounds of lead and brass straight into his breastplate with an unadorned, sickening crunch.

The soldier dropped.

Vex reset her heels, ears flicking back against a sudden, piercing whine that wasn’t artillery.

It was coming from inside her own chest—a hollow, sickening pressure that seized her sternum like an iron hook and hauled backward.

Throw wide the gates…

The voice rattled the teeth in her jaw. It sounded like a man shouting down the mouth of a dry well, distorted and breathless.

Her stomach dropped through the floorboards. She had seen this. She had watched Thancred go limp in the dust. She had watched Urianger, Y’shtola, and Alphinaud drop like puppets with cut strings, their breathing bodies shipped back to the Rising Stones while their souls vanished into the quiet. And then Alisaie—barely three bells ago, collapsing into the red mud right in front of her, sword tumbling from slack fingers.

Not me, she thought, baring her fangs against the vacuum in her ribs. Not while the trench is open.

“Raubahn—” she started, turning her heel.

The air tore.

The trench, the mud, the sulfur, the war—everything bleached into blinding white static. The drag took her by the marrow, hauling her upward with concussive, bone-cracking violence. Her boots left the dirt. She reached out, claws digging uselessly into empty air, before the entire world fell away into total, suffocating silence.

The transition ended with all the grace of a dropped sack of grain.

Vex hit the deck hard. Her left shoulder took the impact, the lead in her soles dragging her into an aggressive combat roll across freezing stone before her knuckles slammed down to break her slide.

She came up crouching on the balls of her feet, brass dusters raised to guard her throat, fangs bared in a feral hiss.

The air was wrong. It didn’t taste like fuel or blood. It tasted like ozone, old paper, and distilled water. Beneath her palms lay tiles of polished azure glass, humming with faint, subterranean light. Above her, ribbed arches of crystal caught a harsh, flat daylight that had no business being so bright.

A dozen paces away stood a figure draped in yards of crimson wool, leaning lightly on a carved ash staff. His face was lost in the deep shadow of an ornate cowl, but the posture was deliberate—regal, ancient, and practiced down to the millimeter.

He did not rush to close the distance. He stood straight, one hand resting on the ash staff, his voice carrying the deep, even resonance of a man accustomed to addressing quiet halls.

“You crossed whole,” he said, the words level and unadorned. “Good. Welcome to the First, Champion. I endeavored to make the landing as gentle as possible, though I see the rift had its own opinions on the matter. I have need…”

As he spoke, Vex’s ears swiveled forward, twitched once at the cadence, and flattened to her skull.

She stood up from her crouch, let her hands drop into her deep vest pockets, and locked her mismatched eyes—molten amber and flinty violet—straight onto the dark hollow beneath his hood.

“Shut up,” she said, her rough Ala Mhigan contralto cutting right through his grand address. “I know it’s you under that stupid hood, G’raha Tia.”

The silence that followed was absolute. And as thick as a thousand year old tree.
